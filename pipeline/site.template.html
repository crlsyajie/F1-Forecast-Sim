How the forecast is made

  1. Training data comes from FastF1: every race from to , one row per driver. Inputs are that weekend's qualifying gap to pole, grid slot, the team's best qualifying gap, whether it rained, and air temperature. The target is the driver's median green-flag race lap, as % slower than the fastest driver (notebook cell 20).
  2. XGBoost settings, monotone constraints, the ×10 scaling of qualifying gap and grid slot, and the race-grouped 80/20 split are copied from cells 22–26. The trained trees run in your browser, so every input change reruns the same model.
  3. Predicted lap = wet-adjusted pole time × (median race-pace-to-pole ratio in the training races) × (1 + predicted %).
  4. At 70% chance of rain or more, qualifying times are multiplied by each driver's wet factor before gaps are computed (cell 17).
  5. In the notebook the regulation-change boost is calculated but never reaches the model. Here it is added after the model, scaled by the boost weight.
  6. The simulator moves each car at its predicted lap time, with a slower first lap and a grid-slot head start. It has no pit stops, safety cars or tyre wear, so it shows pace, not strategy.
  7. Upcoming rounds after the next race have no qualifying yet. They use the Inputs grid and the circuit's 2025 pole time as placeholders.
